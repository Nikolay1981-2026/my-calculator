Is It Better to Rent or Buy a House?

This is one of the most common financial questions. The answer depends on how long you plan to stay in the home, local real estate prices, and current mortgage rates. Many people assume buying is always better, but renting can actually be cheaper in the short term.

When you buy a home, you don't just pay the mortgage. You also pay property taxes, homeowners insurance, maintenance, and closing costs. This calculator helps you see the true cost of homeownership compared to your current rent.

The Hidden Costs of Buying a Home

  • Property Taxes: Usually 0.5% to 2% of the home's value every year.
  • Homeowners Insurance: Required by lenders, covering fire, theft, and liability.
  • Maintenance & Repairs: A good rule of thumb is to budget 1% of the home's value annually for upkeep (roof, HVAC, plumbing).
  • Closing Costs: 2% to 5% of the purchase price paid upfront.
  • Opportunity Cost: The down payment money could have been invested in the stock market instead.

When Does Buying Make Sense?

Buying usually makes more financial sense if:

  • You plan to stay in the home for at least 5 to 7 years (to recoup closing costs and build equity).
  • Home prices are rising steadily in your area.
  • You have a stable income and an emergency fund for repairs.

Rent vs Buy — FAQ

How many years should I stay in a house to make buying worth it?

As a general rule, you should plan to stay for at least 5 to 7 years. This gives you enough time to build equity and offset the upfront costs of buying (closing costs, moving, etc.).

Is renting throwing money away?

No. Renting gives you flexibility and saves you from maintenance costs, property taxes, and the risk of a housing market crash. The money you save by renting can be invested elsewhere.

What is the 5% rule in real estate?

It's a quick way to compare renting vs buying. If the annual cost of owning (mortgage interest, taxes, maintenance) is more than 5% of the home's value, renting might be the better financial choice.
