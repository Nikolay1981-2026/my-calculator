Self-Employed Mortgage — FAQ

Can self-employed people get a mortgage in 2026?

Yes. Lenders work with freelancers, business owners, gig workers, and independent contractors every day. The process is different from a W-2 employee, but self-employed borrowers are a large and growing part of the mortgage market.

How is self-employed income calculated for a mortgage?

Most lenders take your adjusted gross income from the last two tax returns, average it, and divide by 12. If your income grew, they may use the lower year to be conservative. Some lenders offer "bank statement loans" that use 12–24 months of deposits instead of tax returns.

What documents do I need as a self-employed borrower?

Usually two years of personal tax returns, two years of business tax returns (if you have an LLC or S-Corp), a year-to-date profit-and-loss statement, and proof of business ownership. Having these ready speeds up approval.

How much can a freelancer borrow for a mortgage?

It depends on your average income over two years and your debt-to-income ratio. Most lenders allow up to 43% DTI. Our calculator above shows your estimated borrowing power based on your specific numbers.

Do self-employed borrowers pay higher interest rates?

Not necessarily. If your credit score is strong and your income documentation is solid, you can get the same rates as W-2 employees. Some niche loan programs (like bank statement loans) have slightly higher rates because they carry more risk for the lender.

What if my income dropped last year?

Lenders may use the lower of the two years, which reduces how much you can borrow. If your income dropped temporarily (for example, due to a one-time expense), you can explain it with a letter of explanation. Some lenders will consider the higher year in special cases.
