How Do Property Taxes Work?

Property tax is an annual tax paid to your local government (county, city, or school district) based on the assessed value of your real estate. Unlike federal income tax, property tax rates are set locally, which is why they vary wildly from state to state.

Your mortgage lender usually collects a portion of your property tax every month in an escrow account and pays the county on your behalf. This is why the "monthly cost" shown above is important — it gets added to your principal and interest payment.

Highest and Lowest Property Tax States

Effective property tax rates (tax paid as a percentage of home value) range from under 0.3% to nearly 2%. Below is a quick comparison:

What Factors Affect Your Property Tax?

  • Assessed Value: Local authorities determine what your home is worth for tax purposes. This isn't always the same as the market price you paid.
  • Exemptions: Many states offer a "Homestead Exemption" for your primary residence, which lowers the taxable value.
  • Local Millage Rates: Taxes are often calculated in "mills" (one mill = $1 per $1,000 of assessed value). Voters often approve new millage rates for schools or infrastructure.

Property Tax — FAQ

Are property taxes paid monthly or annually?

You can usually pay annually or in installments directly to your county. However, if you have a mortgage, the lender typically collects 1/12th of the bill each month in escrow and pays it for you.

Can property taxes be deducted from federal income tax?

Yes, if you itemize your deductions, you can deduct state and local property taxes up to the SALT cap ($10,000 for most filers, though this may change with new legislation).

What happens if I disagree with my assessed value?

You have the right to appeal your property tax assessment. Contact your local county assessor's office to file an appeal, often requiring proof (like an independent appraisal) that your home is overvalued.
