How to Pay Off Your Mortgage Faster

Paying off your mortgage early is one of the best ways to secure your financial future. Even a small extra payment each month can shave years off your loan and save you tens of thousands of dollars in interest.

Example: Paying off a $70,000 Mortgage

Let's say you have a $70,000 balance at a 6.5% interest rate with 15 years left. Your base monthly payment would be around $610. Over the life of the loan, you would pay roughly $39,700 in interest alone.

If you add just $100 extra per month (paying $710 total), you would pay off the loan nearly 3 years earlier and save over $8,000 in interest. If you can afford $200 extra, you'd save even more.

Bi-Weekly Payments vs. Extra Monthly Payments

There are two main ways to accelerate your payoff:

  • Extra Monthly Payments: This is the simplest method. You just add a fixed amount (e.g., $100 or $200) to your regular payment and instruct the lender to apply it to the principal.
  • Bi-Weekly Payments: Instead of paying once a month, you pay half your mortgage payment every two weeks. Since there are 52 weeks in a year, this results in 26 half-payments, which equals 13 full payments. You essentially make one extra full payment per year without feeling the pinch.

Should You Pay Off Your Mortgage or Invest?

This is a common debate. Paying off a 6.5% mortgage gives you a guaranteed 6.5% return on your money (since you avoid paying that interest). However, if you can earn 8-10% in the stock market, investing might mathematically be a better choice. From a psychological standpoint, though, many people prefer the peace of mind of owning their home outright.

Mortgage Payoff — FAQ

Do extra payments go to principal?

Yes, but only if you specify it. When you make an extra payment, always tell your lender to apply it directly to the principal balance, not to next month's interest.

Are there prepayment penalties?

Most modern mortgages (especially conventional and FHA loans) do not have prepayment penalties. However, it's always best to check your loan documents before making large lump-sum payments.

Can I pay off my mortgage in a lump sum?

Yes. If you receive a windfall (inheritance, bonus, sale of another property), you can often pay off the entire remaining balance. You'll just need to request a "payoff statement" from your lender.
