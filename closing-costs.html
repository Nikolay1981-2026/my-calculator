Closing Costs — FAQ

What exactly are closing costs?

Closing costs are all the fees you pay when you finalize a home purchase. They include the loan origination fee, appraisal, title search and title insurance, attorney fees, recording fees, prepaid property taxes and homeowners insurance, and HOA transfer fees. They are separate from your down payment.

How much are closing costs in 2026?

Most buyers pay 2% to 5% of the home price in closing costs. On a $400,000 home, that is $8,000 to $20,000. The exact amount depends on your state, lender, loan type, and whether you buy points.

Who pays closing costs — buyer or seller?

Typically the buyer pays most closing costs, but the seller pays the real estate agent commissions and sometimes a portion of the buyer's costs. In a slow market, buyers can negotiate for the seller to cover some or all closing costs.

Can closing costs be rolled into the loan?

Sometimes. With certain loan programs (like FHA or VA), you can finance some closing costs into the loan. But this increases your loan balance and total interest paid. Ask your lender for options.

Which states have the highest closing costs?

High-cost states like New York, California, and Washington tend to have higher closing costs because of attorney fees, title insurance rates, and local taxes. Low-cost states like Nevada, Arizona, and Texas have lower average fees.

How can I reduce my closing costs?

Several ways: negotiate the origination fee with your lender, shop around for title insurance, ask the seller to cover a portion, close near the end of the month to reduce prepaid interest, and avoid buying discount points unless you plan to stay long-term.
