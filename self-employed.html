How to Get a Mortgage When You're Self-Employed

Getting a mortgage as a freelancer, business owner, or gig worker is entirely possible. In fact, self-employed borrowers make up a massive portion of the US housing market. However, because your income isn't reported on a simple W-2, lenders have to look at your finances differently.

The Document Checklist

When you apply for a self-employed mortgage, the lender will ask for a specific paper trail to prove your income is stable. Expect to provide:

  • Two years of personal tax returns (Form 1040). Lenders look at your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI).
  • Two years of business tax returns (if you are an LLC, S-Corp, or partnership).
  • A Year-to-Date Profit & Loss (P&L) statement prepared by you or your accountant.
  • Proof of business ownership (business license, CPA letter, or Articles of Organization).

What is a Bank Statement Loan?

If your tax returns show a lot of write-offs (which lowers your AGI and makes it hard to qualify for a large loan), you might need a Bank Statement Loan. Instead of tax returns, the lender looks at 12 to 24 months of your personal or business bank statements to calculate your average monthly deposits. These loans usually require a higher down payment and have slightly higher interest rates, but they are a lifesaver for freelancers with heavy deductions.

How the 2-Year Average Works

Most lenders will take your net income from Year 1 and Year 2, add them together, and divide by 24 to get your qualifying monthly income. If your income went up significantly in Year 2, some lenders will still only use Year 1's lower number to be safe. If you want to maximize your borrowing power, keeping your tax returns clean for two years before applying is the best strategy.

Self-Employed Mortgage — FAQ

Can self-employed people get a mortgage in 2026?

Yes. Lenders work with freelancers, business owners, gig workers, and independent contractors every day. The process is different from a W-2 employee, but self-employed borrowers are a large part of the mortgage market.

How is self-employed income calculated for a mortgage?

Most lenders take your adjusted gross income from the last two tax returns, average it, and divide by 12. If your income grew, they may use the lower year to be conservative. Some lenders offer "bank statement loans" that use 12–24 months of deposits instead of tax returns.

Do self-employed borrowers pay higher interest rates?

Not necessarily. If your credit score is strong and your income documentation is solid, you can get the same rates as W-2 employees. Some niche loan programs (like bank statement loans) have slightly higher rates because they carry more risk for the lender.
