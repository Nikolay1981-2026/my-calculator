Is Refinancing Worth It in 2026?

Refinancing your mortgage means replacing your current loan with a new one, usually to get a lower interest rate, change the loan term, or access home equity. But it's not free — closing costs usually run 2% to 5% of the loan balance. So how do you know if it's worth it?

The Break-Even Rule

The most important number in refinancing is the break-even point. This is how many months it takes for your monthly savings to cover the cost of the refinance.

Example: If refinancing saves you $150 per month and costs $6,000, your break-even point is 40 months (about 3.3 years). If you plan to stay in the home longer than that, refinancing makes sense. If you plan to move in 2 years, you would lose money.

When Should You Refinance?

  • Rates have dropped: A common rule of thumb is to refinance if you can lower your rate by at least 0.75% to 1%.
  • You want to drop PMI: If your home value has increased and you now have 20% equity, refinancing can remove Private Mortgage Insurance.
  • You want to shorten your term: Refinancing from a 30-year to a 15-year loan will increase your monthly payment but save you tens of thousands in interest.
  • You need cash: A cash-out refinance lets you borrow against your home equity, often at a lower rate than a personal loan.

What Are the Costs?

Just like a purchase mortgage, a refinance comes with closing costs. These include application fees, loan origination fees, appraisal fees, title search and insurance, and recording fees. Always ask your lender for a Loan Estimate to see the exact costs before you commit.

Refinance — FAQ

How much does it cost to refinance?

Typically 2% to 5% of the loan balance. On a $300,000 mortgage, that's $6,000 to $15,000. Some lenders offer "no-closing-cost" refinances, but they usually charge a higher interest rate to make up for it.

How long does a refinance take?

Usually 30 to 45 days from application to closing. It can take longer if there are issues with the appraisal or title search.

Can I refinance with bad credit?

It's harder, but yes. FHA and VA loans have more flexible credit requirements. However, you'll likely pay a higher interest rate than someone with excellent credit.
